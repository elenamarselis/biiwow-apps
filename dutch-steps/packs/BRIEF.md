# Dutch Steps — content brief (biiwow app teaching Dutch to Russian speakers)

Format: packs/VOCAB_SCHEMA.md and packs/GRAMMAR_SCHEMA.md in this folder. Validators: `node packs/vcheck.js <LEVEL>` and `node packs/gcheck.js <LEVEL>`.

## Dutch-specific rules
Nouns WITH the definite article de/het ("de tafel", "het huis"); give the plural in forms ("mv. tafels"), and the diminutive when common ("mv. huizen; verkl. huisje"). Verbs in the infinitive; give forms: past singular and participle with heeft/is ("liep, heeft/is gelopen"); mark separable verbs ("belt op, belde op, heeft opgebeld") and fixed prepositions ("wachten op"). Adjectives: give the inflected form when it changes spelling ("groot – grote"). IPA: standard Netherlands Dutch. note_ru: false friends with Russian and German, de/het, Flemish (Belgian) variants.

## Grammar sections per level (10 sections, in this order; ids kebab-case)

### A1
1. Pronunciation and spelling (g/ch, ui, eu, ij/ei, ou/au, long and short vowels, open and closed syllables)
2. Nouns: de and het; plural -en and -s; the indefinite article een
3. Personal pronouns (stressed and unstressed: jij/je, zij/ze, wij/we); zijn and hebben
4. Present tense of regular verbs, spelling changes (ik woon – wij wonen) and jij-inversion
5. Word order: verb second, inversion, question words and yes/no questions
6. Negation: niet vs geen
7. Possessive pronouns (mijn, jouw, zijn, haar, ons/onze, jullie, hun, uw)
8. Modal verbs (kunnen, moeten, willen, mogen) with the infinitive at the end
9. Separable verbs (opbellen, meenemen) and the imperative
10. Numbers, time, dates; er is / er zijn; adjectives with and without -e

### A2
1. Perfectum with hebben and zijn; regular participles and the 't kofschip rule
2. Irregular participles and strong verbs
3. Imperfectum (simple past) of regular and irregular verbs; imperfectum vs perfectum
4. The adjective ending -e: de-words, het-words, een + het-word
5. Comparative and superlative (groter dan, het grootst, beter, meer, meest)
6. Object pronouns (me/mij, je/jou, hem, haar, ons, hen/hun/ze) and reflexive verbs (zich wassen)
7. Subordinate clauses with omdat, dat, als, of, wanneer: verb at the end
8. Coordinating conjunctions (en, maar, of, want, dus) and adverbs that invert (daarom, toch, dan)
9. The future: gaan + infinitive and zullen; te + infinitive (proberen te, beginnen te)
10. The word er (1): place and er is/zijn; prepositions of place and time

### B1
1. The word er (2): er with numbers (ik heb er twee), er + preposition (erop, eraan), all uses of er
2. Relative clauses: die, dat, wat, waar + preposition
3. Passive voice: worden and zijn in present and past
4. Zou/zouden: politeness, wishes, advice; conditional sentences with als
5. Plusquamperfectum (had gedaan, was gegaan) and narration
6. Verb clusters at the end of the clause (dat ik heb moeten werken; infinitive instead of participle)
7. Verbs with fixed prepositions (wachten op, denken aan, zich interesseren voor)
8. Infinitive constructions: om … te, zonder … te, in plaats van … te
9. Indirect speech and indirect questions
10. Diminutives (-je, -tje, -pje, -etje, -kje) and their use

### B2
1. Unreal conditionals in the past (als ik het had geweten, zou ik … hebben)
2. Passive advanced: with modal verbs, impersonal passive (er wordt gedanst), alternatives with men/je
3. Modal verbs in subjective meaning (hij moet ziek zijn, zou … hebben, schijnen te, lijken te, blijken te)
4. Advanced subordinating conjunctions (hoewel, tenzij, zodat, nadat, voordat, zodra, zolang, doordat vs omdat)
5. Present and past participles as adjectives (de huilende baby, de gestolen fiets)
6. Word order in the middle field: pronouns, time-manner-place, position of niet, emphasis
7. Separable vs inseparable verbs (voorkomen, overkomen, ondergaan) and meaning changes
8. Nominalisation and formal style (het nemen van besluiten, de uitvoering)
9. Pronominal adverbs advanced: split er … op, waar … aan, hier/daar + preposition
10. Linking words and text structure (bovendien, echter, daarentegen, kortom, dus)

### C1
1. Fixed verb-noun combinations (in aanmerking komen, ter sprake brengen, rekening houden met)
2. Modal particles (toch, maar, even, eens, wel, nou, hoor, zeg)
3. Extended adjective phrases (de door de minister voorgestelde maatregel) and their paraphrase
4. Formal prepositions (ten aanzien van, op grond van, ondanks, krachtens, middels)
5. Impersonal and passive-like constructions (het is te hopen dat, valt te verwachten, laten + infinitive)
6. Word formation: compounds, linking -s- and -e(n)-, suffixes (-heid, -ing, -schap, -baar, -loos)
7. Subjunctive remnants and fixed formulas (leve de koning, het zij zo, men neme)
8. Text cohesion: reference, connectors, register in written Dutch
9. Gender nuances: de/het edge cases, collective nouns, hij/zij/het for things
10. Register and variation: Netherlands vs Flanders (gij/ge, aan het + infinitive), colloquial forms

### C2
1. Literary and archaic forms: case remnants (des, der, ten, ter), old subjunctive, formal written style
2. Complex sentence architecture: long verb clusters, nesting, red vs green word order (heb gedaan / gedaan heb)
3. Modality and evidentiality (zou in journalism, naar verluidt, vermoedelijk, blijken te)
4. Fine points of er and pronominal adverbs in complex sentences
5. Prepositional regime (zich beroepen op, afzien van, onderhevig aan)
6. Word formation advanced: be-/ver-/ont-/her- verbs, conversion, neologisms and loanwords
7. Orthography and punctuation: Groene Boekje, compounds and hyphens, tussen-n, apostrophes (auto's, 's)
8. Aspect: aan het + infinitive, zitten/staan/liggen/lopen te + infinitive
9. Discourse and pragmatics: u vs je, directness, understatement, irony
10. Variation and advanced errors: hun hebben, groter als, me boek, dialects, Flemish and Surinamese Dutch

Each grammar section: 6-10 rules with 2-4 examples each; 24-32 exercises mixing mc, fill, order, tf, match (about 35/30/15/10/10%), easy to hard; Russian explanations (always containing Russian words) that point out contrasts with Russian; vary the position of the correct mc option; options of an mc item must differ by more than capitalisation.

## How to work
Write in parts with the Write tool into a unique subfolder of your scratchpad (random suffix; the scratchpad is shared with other agents). After EACH part, concatenate what exists into the pack file with a small node script, so partial work is never lost. Run the validator until it prints OK (vocab) or no ERRORS (grammar), and re-run it right before you report, because other levels are written in parallel.
